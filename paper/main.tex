\begin{abstract}
We investigate whether spatially localized temporal filtering can reduce rapid visual changes while preserving more source information than whole-frame filtering. The artifact implements no-filter, global, and block-localized baselines plus an event-specific adaptive method with separate luminance-transition, saturated-red, and regular-pattern evidence channels. A deterministic 12-scenario synthetic benchmark, a compact parameter sweep, component ablations, and a natural-media case study provide evaluation. At default settings, the adaptive method reduces aggregate mean temporal activity from 0.10866 to 0.02966 and attains mask IoU 0.8907, but incurs MAE 0.05624 and leaves aggregate peak activity 0.16744 because a reversal is required before correction. More importantly, no adaptive configuration lies on the aggregate MAE--activity Pareto frontier; tuned global and localized baselines provide better tradeoffs on these two metrics. A 47-second lyric-video case study reduces mean activity from 0.06293 to 0.01408 while visibly altering the sequence and leaving a filtered peak of 0.11285. These values are computational proxies, not clinical validation, seizure-risk estimates, or guarantees of standards compliance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Rapid changes in video can cover a complete frame or a small object. A global spatial average may dilute an intense localized event, while a whole-frame response may alter content unrelated to that event. This motivates the research question: \emph{at comparable levels of measured temporal suppression, how do global, localized, and event-specific methods differ in preservation of the source video?}

The project is an interpretable testbed rather than a clinical system. It contributes (1) explicit color and temporal-change definitions; (2) three clear baselines; (3) an experimental adaptive method that maps distinct evidence to distinct corrections; (4) controlled synthetic ground truth, per-case metrics, a parameter study, and ablations; and (5) executable scripts, tests, and generated tables. Reconstruction and frame-interpolation filters retained in the repository are exploratory failure investigations, not central methods.

Clinical reviews establish that visually sensitive responses depend on temporal, spatial, chromatic, display, viewing, and individual factors \cite{fisher2005photic,fisher2022visually,harding2010image}. This project measures only decoded-pixel signals. Throughout,
\[
\text{computational temporal activity}\ne\text{clinical seizure risk}.
\]

\section{Related Work}
Clinical and psychophysical literature identifies flashes, long-wavelength red transitions, and high-contrast regular patterns as relevant stimulus classes \cite{fisher2022visually,wilkins2005patterns,porciatti2000cortical}. Broadcast guidance and web accessibility material translate parts of that evidence into operational tests \cite{itu1702,w3c2023flashes}; however, standards-inspired analysis is not equivalent to formal conformance. A recent guideline comparison also shows that definitions and tool behavior vary across regimes \cite{jordan2024guidelines}.

Computational work includes adaptive temporal filtering evaluated with patients \cite{nomura2000adaptive}, automatic flashing-content detection \cite{carreira2015automatic}, parallel detection architectures \cite{alzubaidi2016parallel}, learned video transformations \cite{barbu2020deep}, and user-facing GIF defenses \cite{south2021gifs}. Unlike black-box transformation approaches, this undergraduate project emphasizes small auditable components, spatial ground truth, and the suppression--distortion tradeoff. It does not claim algorithmic novelty or reproduce a normative analyzer in full.

\section{Problem Formulation}
Let a video be $V=\{I_0,\ldots,I_{T-1}\}$ with RGB frames in $[0,1]^{H\times W\times3}$. Decoded values are assumed to use the sRGB encoding curve. Each channel is linearized using the sRGB inverse transfer function, after which relative linear luminance is
\begin{equation}
Y_t=0.2126R_{t,\mathrm{lin}}+0.7152G_{t,\mathrm{lin}}+0.0722B_{t,\mathrm{lin}}.
\end{equation}
sRGB and BT.709 share primaries and a D65 white point, hence the linear-light coefficients; BT.709 itself does not specify sRGB decoding \cite{itu709,w3c_luminance}. Applying these weights directly to encoded RGB is available only as an explicitly named brightness proxy.

Per-pixel change and its global mean are
\begin{align}
\Delta_t(x,y)&=|Y_t(x,y)-Y_{t-1}(x,y)|,\\
D_t&=\frac{1}{HW}\sum_{x,y}\Delta_t(x,y).
\end{align}
If a fraction $p$ changes by $a$, then $D_t=pa$; small regions are therefore diluted. We seek filtered video $\widehat V$ conceptually satisfying
\begin{equation}
\min_{\widehat V}\mathcal D(V,\widehat V)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \mathcal R(\widehat V)\le\tau,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal D$ is source distortion and $\mathcal R$ is the defined temporal-activity proxy. We evaluate both rather than collapsing them into an unsupported safety score.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Baselines}
\textbf{No filtering} copies the source. \textbf{Global filtering} detects $D_t\ge\tau$ and recursively blends the entire current frame toward the prior output. \textbf{Localized block filtering} averages the last $w$ change maps, thresholds fixed block means, optionally closes gaps, and Gaussian-feathers the mask $M_t$. Its output is
\begin{equation}
\widehat I_t=(1-\alpha M_t)I_t+\alpha M_t\widehat I_{t-1}.
\end{equation}
All three are $O(HW)$ per frame. Block size trades spatial precision for robustness, while recursive blending can create motion trails.

\subsection{Event-Specific Adaptive Method}
Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} reflects the implemented pipeline. Separate masks $M_t^L,M_t^R,M_t^P$ encode three kinds of evidence. Luminance and red events require two consecutive, above-threshold signed transitions of opposite direction at the same pixel. The red signal is the documented proxy $R-\max(G,B)$ where $R/(R+G+B)$ exceeds a configured ratio; it is not a normative chromaticity test. Pattern evidence searches tile-wise horizontal and vertical mean profiles for sufficiently strong, alternating, regularly spaced edges.

Each channel receives one motivated correction. Red evidence is desaturated toward an equal-channel value having the same relative linear luminance. At luminance-event pixels,
\begin{equation}
Y_t^*=\operatorname{clip}(Y_t,\widehat Y_{t-1}-s,\widehat Y_{t-1}+s),
\end{equation}
then a neutral linear-RGB offset realizes $Y_t^*$. Pattern contrast is contracted around Gaussian local mean $\mu_t$:
\begin{equation}
I_t^P=\mu_t+(1-\rho)(I_t-\mu_t).
\end{equation}
Gaussian-feathered masks composite each result. Pattern correction starts on frame zero so a static pattern is not given an artificial one-frame transient. Total time is $O(THW)$ and streaming state is $O(HW)$.

\section{Experimental Setup}
The generator uses seed 7 and produces twelve 24-frame, $96\times64$, 24-fps RGB sequences: whole-frame alternation; small, medium, multiple, and rapid localized alternation; static high-contrast texture; gradual illumination; scene cut; global exposure change; moving bright object; localized red alternation; and a persistent regular pattern. Positive cases include exact masks; empty masks mark designed confounds, not medically safe content.

We report mean and peak $D_t$, threshold-exceeding frames, high-change area, residual activity in labeled regions, MAE, MSE, PSNR, SSIM, modified-area ratio, mask precision/recall/F1/IoU, time, and FPS. Infinite aggregate PSNR can occur because no-op cases have zero MSE, so MAE and SSIM are more useful summaries here.

The compact sweep evaluates global thresholds $\{.04,.08,.12,.18\}$ and blends $\{.35,.65,.85\}$; localized thresholds $\{.06,.12,.18\}$, the same blends, and block sizes $\{4,8\}$; and adaptive luminance thresholds $\{.05,.10,.15\}$, maximum steps $\{.03,.08,.15\}$, and moderate/strong red-pattern profiles. Every setting is evaluated on every case. Pareto efficiency minimizes aggregate MAE and mean activity within this finite grid.

The real-media case study is a 47-second, $1280\times720$, 23.976-fps closing excerpt from the official ``BREAK MY SOUL'' lyric video \cite{beyonce2022break}. It was chosen for repeated large transitions, not because it has a clinical label. The source URL, segment, local hash, and rationale are recorded without redistributing the video.

\subsection{Suppression--Distortion Study}
Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} is generated from 588 per-case measurements. The main negative result is that none of the 18 adaptive configurations is non-dominated under aggregate MAE and mean activity. The 17-point frontier contains no filtering, global, and localized settings. For example, the strongest evaluated adaptive setting reaches activity .02315 at MAE .06465; localized $\tau=.06$, $\alpha=.85$, block 8 reaches lower activity .01654 at lower MAE .05128. This does not prove universal inferiority, but it rejects a claim that the current adaptive method offers the best aggregate two-objective tradeoff.

Spatial localization still matters case-by-case. On the small square, the global default makes no change; localized filtering reduces mean activity from .01007 to .00198 while modifying .998\% of pixel locations. Adaptive filtering reaches .00287 with IoU .897, versus .262 for coarse block localization. On whole-frame alternation, localization offers no spatial advantage: global and localized outputs are identical. On localized red alternation, adaptive IoU is .954 but its MAE .08167 exceeds localized MAE .02949.

\subsection{Natural-Media Case Study}
Mean activity in the lyric excerpt falls from .06293 to .01408 (77.6\%). At the strongest source transition it falls from .11880 to .02770, but the maximum elsewhere in the filtered excerpt is .11285. Mean absolute RGB modification is .08955 and 59.6\% of decoded pixel locations change by more than one 8-bit code value. Figure~\ref{fig:lyric-sequence} shows the three strongest adjacent pairs. Black-to-gray changes are plainly visible; the measurement gain is not free.

\section{Ablation Study}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} shows that luminance correction accounts for nearly all aggregate activity suppression. Adding red evidence changes activity from .02985 to .02966 but raises MAE from .03941 to .04281. Pattern correction changes static appearance and increases MAE without reducing temporal activity. The all-channel configuration improves aggregate mask IoU because the synthetic suite includes labeled red and pattern cases, yet it is not the best preservation choice.

\section{Failure Cases and Limitations}
The global and localized baselines respond to an isolated scene cut, modifying 4.17\% and 8.33\% of locations respectively despite an empty event mask. Localized filtering also modifies 2.11\% on a moving bright object; global and adaptive defaults do not. None responds to the designed global exposure change at its default threshold. These results expose the limitations of raw differences and threshold selection.

The adaptive detector requires an opposing transition, so the first large change survives. For whole-frame alternation, its peak remains .75431 even though mean activity falls from .75431 to .10932. Pattern detection is limited to horizontal/vertical tile profiles, its correction can alter a static image without changing temporal activity, and the red ratio is not perceptually uniform. Recursive baselines may ghost moving content; exploratory reconstruction can produce interpolation artifacts. Compression changes decoded pixels and therefore contaminates modification counts.

The dataset is small, synthetic, SDR, and internally uncompressed. Its ground truth is design intent, not medical outcome. The natural-media example has no clinical annotation. The implementation omits calibrated display output, viewing geometry, HDR, individual physiology, full chromaticity rules, scene understanding, and formal validation against independent commercial analyzers. Runtime values depend on hardware. Accordingly, neither a metric threshold nor a filtered output should be described as medically safe.

\section{Conclusion}
Localized filtering demonstrably avoids whole-frame modification on small controlled regions, but the best method depends on the chosen suppression level and distortion budget. The current event-specific adaptive method improves interpretability and localization, yet the parameter study shows that it is not Pareto-superior on aggregate MAE and mean activity. Its luminance branch supplies almost all measured suppression, while red and pattern branches provide scenario-specific behavior at additional distortion cost. The real-media case study confirms substantial mean-activity reduction alongside visible alteration and a remaining large peak. The project therefore establishes a reproducible framework and a set of falsifiable findings, not a finished safety system. Motion compensation, scene-cut rejection, local frequency estimation, stronger chromatic modeling, and held-out parameter selection are the most defensible next experiments.
